# Field research behind the designs

Written when the animation was planned at 8 frames a second: "f" or "fr" = 1/8 s. The animation is now continuous;
read frame counts as timings. FLAG = from a related species, a weak source or an estimate. u: 0 left to 1 right of
the drawing, v: 0 top to 1 bottom. "Mirror for left to right" notes are from before the engine did that for you.

## Land

**malayan-tapir** (done in ref.js)

**malayan-tiger**: Cats walk in lateral sequence (hind then fore on the same side), head level and low, shoulder
blades rolling above the back line. Scent-marks mainly by spraying urine (marks 42 cm to 1.7 m up) and scraping;
flehmen held several seconds. Strong swimmer. Stride ~0.8–1 per s (FLAG). PASS (5 s, heaviest, most unhurried cat):
10 f stride; shoulder region (u 0.2–0.4, v 0.1–0.3) rises 2% while the near foreleg is planted and drops as it swings
(the shoulder roll); head band level, dips 2° at each fore footfall; tail (u>0.75) hangs in a shallow J, its last
15% flicks up 10° every 2 strides; tall ink grass blades in the foreground bend 15° away from the body as it passes,
spring back. OUTING: walks out, turns, backs up to an ink tree trunk; tail swings up to vertical about its base (70°);
a fan of fine droplets sprays back and up onto the trunk at 0.4–1.0 of body height; two hind-leg scrapes (hind band
shears 6% back) with dirt puffs; flehmen: head up 6°, held 2 s with nothing else moving; walks home. Signature: tail
straight up, spray mist on the trunk.

**bornean-orangutan**: Bornean females: clambering 51%, vertical climbing 18%, quadrupedal walking 12%, brachiation
11%, tree-sway 7%. Slow, deliberate, all four hands. Tree-sway: rocks a flexible trunk with growing amplitude to cross
a gap (Thorpe 2007). Kiss-squeak: sharp intake through pursed lips; some Bornean orangutans hold leaves to the lips
(lowers the pitch). PASS: hand-over-hand along an extra ink liana spanning the screen; the whole drawing swings as a
pendulum about the grip hand (top right on the bar) ±8°, half-swings ~1.5 s; leg band (v>0.7) lags 2 f; at each
forward apex the free arm rotates up 60° about its shoulder to reach; mirror so the reaching hand becomes the grip
hand; 3 regrips; each grip shakes 3–5 leaves off, fluttering down. OUTING: tree-sway beat: amplitude grows 3°, 6°,
10°, 14° on a 2 s period, regrips on the biggest swing and travels; stops; free arm brings a hand to the mouth, an ink
leaf at the lips, two pursed-lip puffs (kiss-squeak), the leaf drops; swings home. Signature: the leaf kiss-squeak.

**malayan-sun-bear**: Smallest bear; feet turn inward (pigeon-toed); often stands upright; excellent climber; rips
trunks open with long claws for bees, termites, grubs; tongue 20–25 cm. (Upright walking is short distances only.)
PASS (front view crossing sideways): rolling bipedal waddle; the whole drawing rocks ±4° about the planted foot,
switching foot every 0.5 s, translating once per step; leg bands (v>0.75) shear 3% inward on each swing; arms swing
opposite the legs; head bobs 1%; 4–5 ink bees loop round the head, one trailing. OUTING: waddles to an ink stump;
nose-up sniff (head back 4°, 3 bobs); arms rake down alternately (30° about the shoulders, 3 strokes/s), wood chips
and termites burst out; tongue out as an ink line from the mouth to ~1/4 body width, licks twice, retracts; sits back
(squash 5%); waddles home. Signature: the long tongue.

**sunda-pangolin**: Most arboreal pangolin; semi-prehensile tail with a bare tip pad. Walks on all fours, head and
tail low (humped); forefeet on their outer edges, claws tucked. Rolls into a ball when threatened, scales up.
(Bipedal walking is documented for ground pangolins, not this one: not used.) PASS: slow stiff shuffle, 0.75 s
stride; fore leg band (u 0.2–0.35, v>0.75) shears only 2% (knuckle-walking), hind 4%; leaf-litter flicks and
scattering ant dots at each footfall. OUTING: shuffles to an ink termite mound; head dips 8° and nods in 4 times,
soil crumbs flying back; a falling leaf hits it; it curls (head tucks 40° down, tail swings ~100° forward about the
hip, whole drawing rolls one turn), holds 1.5 s, uncurls, shuffles home. Signature: rolling into a ball.

**lesser-mouse-deer**: ~45 cm, 2 kg; active by day; tiptoe walk (FLAG). Alarm: hind-foot stamping, up to ~7/s in
the Java mouse-deer (FLAG, related species); freeze then zigzag bolt (FLAG). Sang Kancil. PASS: three ~0.6 s darts
separated by 0.5 s freezes (truly still); during a dart legs (v>0.65) blur (fast ±10% shear), body tilts 2°
nose-down, hops 1.5%; after each freeze the head snaps up 4°; leaf specks fly up in darts and settle in freezes.
OUTING: tiptoe walk (3% shear); freezes 1.5 s; hind legs drum fast for 1 s with tick marks under the hoof; zigzag
dash (lanes switch 3 times, scale 0.9/1.0 for depth); tiptoes home. Signature: the freeze, then the drum.

**bornean-pygmy-elephant**: Lateral-sequence footfalls at every speed, never gallops; at ~1.1 m/s stride ~2.3 s.
Ear flapping to cool. Family groups; swims. PASS (4.5 s, two strides ~2.2 s each): leg sub-bands shear ±5% in the
order left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore, ~0.5 s apart, feet planted; body sinks 1% and head dips 1.5° on
each fore footfall; trunk band (u<0.15) swings ±6° as a pendulum lagging the stride by 0.5 s; one slow ear fan (8°
forward and back over 1.5 s); tail ±5°; heavy dust puffs at each forefoot. OUTING: walks out; trunk lifts 60° over
0.5 s, a second sub-band curls the tip into an S to sniff the air, swaying 1.5 s; lowers, scoops and flings a halftone
dust cloud over its back (FLAG); two slow ear flaps; walks home. Signature: slow ear fan with the trunk raised to sniff.

**gaur**: Bulls 1.8–1.9 m at the shoulder, 1,000–1,500 kg; shoulder ridge up to ~13 cm above the rump. Timid;
whistling snort alarm; crashes away surprisingly fast. Bulls threaten with horns low, thump the ground, snort (FLAG);
side-on display (FLAG). PASS: calm, then bolt: 1.2 s heavy walk (3% shear), head jerks up 6°, two snort puffs from
the nostrils, then a rocking-horse gallop: body pitches ±3° every 0.4 s, bounds 2%, forelegs shear ±9% as a pair then
hind legs as a pair; crosses the rest in 2 s; thick dust clouds and flying twigs. OUTING: walks out, stops broadside;
head band rotates 10° down (horns low), rear squashes 3%, holds 1.5 s; foreleg lifts and stamps twice with dust rings,
snort puff; head back up; walks home measured. Signature: the slow head-lowering under the towering ridge.

**sunda-clouded-leopard**: Hind ankles rotate back, so it descends trunks head first, climbs upside down under
branches, hangs by its hind feet (only the margay can too). Very long tail used as a balancing pole. PASS: head-first
descent: an ink trunk running top right to bottom left at ~35°; drawing rotated 30° nose-down, travels down it in a
1 s careful stride (forelegs reach down 5%, hind band braces); tail raised, swinging ±8° opposite the head dips;
drops off at the bottom rotating back to 0°; bark flakes and leaves knocked loose. (Note for a left-to-right pass:
the trunk runs top left to bottom right.) OUTING: walks out along an ink branch, tightrope style: body tilts ±2°, tail
swings ±12° the other way each step; the trick: rotates 150° about the hind feet over 0.5 s, hangs upside down 1.5 s
swatting a falling leaf with a foreleg; swings back up; returns. Signature: the tail as a balancing pole.

**asian-water-monitor** (top view): Tail ~50% of length. Swimming: legs pressed to the body, waves from the tail base
(near third ~60% of thrust). Forked tongue flicks. Basks. Walking: S-bending spine, diagonal legs together (FLAG,
general lizard). PASS: first half on land: trunk band bends ±8° about mid-body, hips the other way; front-left and
hind-right legs rotate 20° forward together, then the other pair, 0.5 s each; head band counter-rotates so the head
stays on the path; forked tongue (ink V) out 0.25 s of every 0.75 s. Mid-screen it crosses an ink shoreline: legs
rotate back flat against the body, a travelling wave runs through 3–4 tail sub-bands, phase-shifted, growing to the
tip, 0.75 s period; ink V-wake and ripples from the tail. OUTING: S-path wander; stops, raises head (head band 1.05),
sweeps 15° side to side, 3 tongue flicks; basks: legs splay wider, body flattens (x 1.03), holds 2 s; walks back.
Signature: the forked tongue over the S-wave.

**smooth-coated-otter**: Strong swimmer (feet paddle slow; tail and hind legs drive fast). Humped bounding lope on
land (rear feet together, then front feet). Periscopes up in water. Family groups. PASS: humped bound, 2 bounds/s:
stretched (x 1.04) → airborne (lifted 3%, nose down 3°) → humped (back u 0.4–0.6, v<0.4 raised 4%, hind legs shear
forward, forelegs back) → front landing; tail trails behind; in the last second it dives nose-down 35° into an ink
water band: crown of splash droplets and rings. OUTING: swims with only head and back above an ink waterline, back
band undulating; periscopes: rotates 70° upright about the tail base, head scans 10° side to side, drops fall; dives
tail last; climbs out and shakes (±3° fast for 1 s) with a fan of spray; returns. Signature: the periscope.

**saltwater-crocodile**: Land gaits: belly slide; high walk (body lifted, legs symmetrical, tail tip dragging);
rare gallop (juveniles); low sprawl. In water: paddles slow, or tucks legs and drives with tail S-waves. Gapes ashore
for long periods. Trackways: clawed prints and a tail-drag groove. PASS (5 s, low and slow): first third belly slide
(belly v 0.6–0.8 on the ground line; legs each side push back with 6% shear, body lurches forward in steps); then it
rises into a high walk (leg band v>0.7 stretches 1.2x vertically over 0.5 s), diagonal pairs step in a 1.25 s stride,
tail tip dragging; leaves a trackway: two rows of clawed prints, a wavy tail groove, a smooth patch where it slid.
OUTING: high-walks out, stops; lower jaw (u<0.25, v 0.5–0.65) opens 25° about the hinge over 0.4 s, holds dead
still for 3 s, snaps shut in one frame with snap marks; belly-slides home. Signature: the long gape and the snap.

**malayan-peacock-pheasant**: Shy forest-floor walker; slips into cover rather than flies. Display (BirdLife): tail
fanned like a peacock's, mantle raised, body flattened low; ruffled head; athletic jumps. Titbitting (pick up food,
shake head, drop it, spread tail toward the ground; FLAG, genus). Head-bobbing walk (general galliform). PASS: quick
light steps, 0.4 s each; head holds still in space while the body slides, then thrusts forward; legs shear 6%; tail
low, sways ±2°; mid-pass a 1 s display burst: whole bird squashes 8% down, the tail band rotates up toward the viewer
and scales 1.3x vertically so the eyespots open into a fan, mantle lifts 3%, hops 6% and runs on; litter flicks behind
each step. OUTING: scratch-forages (one leg shears 8% back with a litter flick, two pecks with the head 25° down);
lifts a morsel, shakes its head 3 times, drops it; low fan display held 2 s, eyespots glint; jumps; walks home.
Signature: the low flattened fan with eyespots facing the viewer.

## Canopy and air

**sunda-slow-loris**: Never leaps; slow fluid movement with three limbs holding; captive Sunda slow lorises:
suspension 29%, quadrupedal walking 24%, bridging 23%, climbing 21%; freezes (rigid holds ≥3 s). Defence: raises
arms over the head and licks the brachial gland (venom). Stalks insects slowly. PASS (5 s, the longest): along an
extra ink branch continuing its own diagonal branch; one limb at a time (each limb sub-band shears 5% forward while
the other three stay planted), a 4-beat 1 s cycle, gliding along the branch axis; a 1.5 s dead-still freeze at the
midpoint; an ink moth flutters ahead along the branch. OUTING: creeps out; the moth lands; freeze 3 s; the only fast
move: forearm rotates 30° forward in one frame, the moth vanishes; startled: both arms rotate ~120° up over the head
in 0.5 s, head tucks 10°, a small tongue tick at the inner elbow twice; holds 1.5 s, lowers arms slowly, creeps home.

**binturong**: One of two carnivores with a prehensile tail (near body length), used as a fifth limb. Slow climber,
stiff bear-like amble, rarely leaps; hind paws rotate for head-first descent. Gland under the tail smells of popcorn
(2-acetyl-1-pyrroline) as the tail drags. Tail-hanging (FLAG). PASS: tail-anchored crawl along an extra branch line,
1.5 s cycle: (a) tail tip tightens its curl 15° round the branch; (b) two stiff steps (legs shear 4%, body bobs 2%);
(c) tail releases and slides forward (tail band stretches 5%); (d) tail regrips. Little popcorn puffs rise and fade
along the tail's drag line. OUTING: ambles out, tail wraps; swings down about the tail grip to hang head-down 1.5 s
(FLAG), forepaws grab an ink fig; swings back up, sits and nods 3 times chewing; drags its tail home with puffs.

**proboscis-monkey**: Habitually wades upright (Britannica); strong swimmer; crosses rivers by leaping from high
branches and belly-flopping (height 10–15 m, popular sources). Male's nose resonates honks to attract females and see
off rivals, evenings by rivers. PASS: crouch (squash 6% for 0.4 s), launch on a parabolic arc across two-thirds of
the screen; in flight the body rotates up to 25° forward and stretches 1.05x, arms reach forward, tail streams up
behind; lands belly-first on an ink waterline: big splash crown, droplets, rings; dog-paddles out with the upper
third showing, arms rotating ±20° alternately every 0.5 s. The drawn branch is a prop (layer 1): leave it behind or
hide it. OUTING: hops branch to branch and sits; honk display: nose (u 0.38–0.52, v 0.12–0.3 on this drawing; check
the grid) swings 8° out and up and back while the chin drops 3°; three honks ~0.5 s apart with ink sound arcs; tail
sways ±4° on a 2 s period; hops home.

**white-handed-gibbon**: Brachiation is up to 80% of locomotion: slow (always a handhold, pendulum) and fast
ricochetal (a parabolic flight between handholds); controls speed by when it lets go. Swings 12–15 m, leaps 8–9 m,
up to ~50 km/h (popular). ~0.9 s per handhold (estimate). Pairs duet; the female's great call accelerates, often with
vigorous swinging and branch-shaking (FLAG). PASS (4 s, 4 strides of 1 s): hide the drawn bar (prop layer 0) and draw
an ink vine across the screen at grip height; each stride: body below the wrist rotates about the grip point (37%
across, at the top) from −35° to +30° with the pivot fixed on the vine, the leg band lags 6% (the whip); release: a
parabola carries it +12% of screen width, rising 4% then falling; catch: mirror so the grip is now at 63% (the other
hand); the vine dips 3% and wobbles. Stride 1 pure pendulum; later strides add flight, so it speeds up. Two leaves
knocked loose at each catch. OUTING: 0–2 s hangs and sings: rising "ooo" note marks whose spacing shrinks; 2–6 s
climax: fast swinging out and back along the vine, 3 strides each way, waves running along the vine; one big ricochet
leap to a lower vine ~20% of screen width away; 6–9 s swings home, slowing to damped swings ±12°, ±6°, ±2°.
Signature: the change of hands on each catch, with flight gaps.

**rhinoceros-hornbill**: "Flap-flap-flap-glide"; wingbeats famously loud ("locomotive", "whoosh"); pairs call in
flight; glides with the primaries splayed and curled up (great hornbill). Wingbeat ~3–4 Hz (estimate). PASS (4.5 s):
3 beats at ~2.7 Hz (wing region rotates about the shoulder +35° up, −20° down), then a 0.75 s glide; repeat; in the
glide the rear 25% of the wing curls up 6%; body pitches 3° nose-down on each downbeat, tail lags; path rises 2% over
each flap burst and sinks 4% in each glide (a heavy, slowly falling wave); on every downbeat 2 curved whoosh streaks
behind the wing, fading; two leaves shaken loose near the top edge. OUTING: lifts the bill 15° twice with "gok" call
ticks; crouches (squash 5%) and drops off (the branch stays: prop layer 3); flies a slow oval across the upper screen
in the flap-glide rhythm; at the far end turns (mirror) during a glide, a brief squash to fake the bank; brakes with
wings held up +40°, lands on the branch, settles with a bob. Signature: three heavy downbeats with whoosh streaks,
then the glide with up-curled tips.

**oriental-pied-hornbill**: A few rapid beats then a rigid glide; noisy, flocks of 3–40 (up to 170 at Khao Yai);
forest edges, gardens. Tongue too short to move food: tosses food back with a jerk of the head, even into the air.
PASS (3.5 s, livelier): 4 beats at ~4 Hz (+30°/−25°), then a 0.5 s glide with the tail band fanned (y 1.15); path
almost flat, 1% bounce per burst; two small simple ink flock-mates (bill + V of wings, ~12% scale) follow above and
behind, out of phase; 3 short "yap" ticks at the bill per burst. OUTING: peers down twice (head −10°); drops off (a
falling fig hides the take-off; branch stays); bouncy zigzag to a low point; a fruit (ink circle) appears at the bill
tip; flies back and lands; the toss: head rotates up 35° about the neck, fruit rises 10% of height, hangs, falls into
the open bill; head snaps back; throat squashes (gulp). Signature: the fruit toss and catch.

**collared-kingfisher**: Perch-and-pounce: sits still for long spells over mud or shallows, swift glide-drop onto
prey (mangroves: mostly crabs; also fish, lizards, insects). Carries prey back and beats it against the branch.
"kee-kee-kee" 3–5 notes. Head-bobbing before a dive (kingfishers generally). Fast direct flight (FLAG). PASS (3 s):
the pounce, drawn big: enters high (15% of screen height), dives at 30° wings folded, nose down 25°, to 85% height at
45% across; strike: a splash of droplets and a ripple ring; climbs out at 15° with a crab glyph in the bill, wings
whirring continuously (no gliding, unlike the hornbills). OUTING: 0–2.5 s frozen except 3 head bobs (head 3% forward
and down, hold) and one tail flick (+8°); dives low left and grabs; returns on a different arc; beats the crab: head
swings ±30° about the neck 3 times with ink specks at each impact; ends calling: 4 ticks, lower bill opening 10°.
Branch is prop layer 4. Signature: beating the crab on the branch.

**buffy-fish-owl**: Smallest fish owl, 40–48 cm; ear tufts point sideways ~45°. Hunts from a perch at the water's
edge, swoops down or wades in, never plunges its body. Fish owls lack the fringes for silent flight (audible
wingbeats); rounded wings. Moves the whole head (fixed eyes) and bobs it for parallax. Dusk and night. PASS (4.5 s):
you pass the owl: the drawing slides across at constant speed like a camera pan; three big foreground leaves cross
at 1.8x that speed (parallax); branch sways ±2° about its left end; head tracks you in three snaps (+15°, 0°, −15°
tilt about the neck at 25%, 50%, 75% across); at 50% one slow blink (eye band squashes to 15% briefly); then a
sideways parallax bob of the head (±4%); ear-tuft tips ruffle. OUTING: two parallax bobs, leans forward (squash 6%),
launches (wings in front view: side bands |u−0.5|>0.3 shear outward 12% and up 6%, slow 2 Hz beat); swoops low and
glides over 2 ink ripple lines; strikes feet first (leg band shears 8% down and forward) with splash droplets and a
fish in the talons; labored climb with the fish dangling; lands with wings spread, the branch dips 3° and springs
back. Branch is prop layer 0. Signature: jerky head snaps, slow blink, sideways bob.

**large-flying-fox**: Wingspan up to ~1.5 m, ~1 kg. Roosts head-down wrapped in its wings; fans wings to cool. Leaves
the roost in long streams at dusk; tracked between roosts up to ~100 km apart. Circles a fruit tree, lands upright on
a branch tip, then flips head-down to feed. Wingbeat ~3 Hz (estimate); bats partly fold the wing on the upstroke.
PASS (5 s, slowest): front view read as flying slightly toward you (scale 0.9→1.05 across), banked 4° toward travel;
steady rowing, no glides, ~2 Hz: up (outer band |x−0.5|>0.25 rises 12% of height and narrows to 85% width toward the
centre: the upstroke fold), mid, down (tips lowered 10% at full span), mid; body bobs opposite the wings; a dusk stream
of 8 tiny "m" glyphs far behind at 30% of its speed, each flapping. OUTING: falls 6% with wings half-folded, then
rows; one elliptical circuit (scale 0.7 on the far side of the loop, for depth); approaches an ink branch line top
right; wings fold to 40% width and it lands upright; flips 180° about its feet and hangs head-down; fans its wings
while 2 fig drops fall; drops off, rotating upright as it falls, and rows home. Signature: the upright landing, then
the flip to hang.

**rajah-brookes-birdwing**: Males fly fast and bird-like with little manoeuvring; crosses a river in 3–4 wingbeats
(anecdotal: long glides). Before settling: glides slowly in tight circles, spiralling down; on landing flutters
nervously for a few seconds, then rests with wings flat, forewings swept back over the hindwings. Puddles at hot
springs and seeps (300+ at Ulu Geroh). Real wingbeat ~11 Hz is too fast to show: draw it as a style. PASS (3.5 s):
top view rotated so the head points the way it travels; a gentle S across the upper half, heading wobbling ±6°;
3 beats (wings squash to ~45% width toward the body centre, then open; scale 0.96/1.0 on each beat), then a long glide
with wings flat; each beat adds a 4% forward surge; sunlit dust motes curl into little vortex swirls behind each beat.
OUTING: fast take-off to the upper centre; a tight spiral down, 2.5 turns, heading turning with it, radius shrinking
from 8% of screen width, scale 1.0→0.75 as it descends away from you, wings flat; lands on a wet ink stain with
ripple rings; nervous flutter (wings to 80% width, rapidly); settles flat, the forewing band rotates 6° back about
the wing root; a straight, fast burst home. Signature: the descending spiral, then the nervous flutter.

**sunda-colugo**: The membrane runs from neck to fingers, toes and tail tip; it cannot flap. Glides usually 30–70 m,
record 145 m; one climbed 320 m and glided 1,342 m in a night. ~8 m of climb buys a 30–50 m glide (ratio ~4–6:1, angle
~9–14°, estimate). Can make 90° turns mid-glide; brakes and alights gently, head-up on a trunk. Climbs by "a series of
lingering hops"; "spun around, stretched her legs and tail, and glided like a magic carpet… then hopped, hopped,
hopped up the tree." PASS (4 s, the only animal with no flapping): rotate the drawing so the head leads; a perfectly
steady straight line from 10% to 45% of screen height (~11°); the only motion: the trailing edge of the membrane
(outer 15%) flutters ±2% fast, plus a ±2° roll wobble each second; 4 long wind streaks parallel to the path; leaves
torn loose in its wake. OUTING: an ink trunk line; the colugo head-up on it with the membrane wrapped (outer band at
55% width); 6 hitching hops up (+4% each, quick, then a hold); spins (scale-x 1→0.2→−1); the membrane snaps open
(scale-x 1.05 overshoot, then 1.0); glides with one banked 90° turn mid-way (scale-x 0.85 in the bank); flare: head
swings back to vertical, membrane billows (x 1.08, y 0.92); lands on the home trunk with a 4% squash; two hops, wraps.

## Reef and open sea

**green-sea-turtle** (top view, head right): "Underwater flight": both front flippers beat together like wings, rear
flippers steer. Cruising stroke ~0.18–0.23 Hz at ~0.6 m/s; thrust in only ~1.4 s of each 4.3 s cycle, the rest a
glide. Faster strokes leaving the surface, slower descending. Rests and grazes seagrass. PASS: straight, slight
upward drift; stroke cycle 2 s: power 0.4 s (both front-flipper regions rotate back ~50° about their shoulders, body
surges forward), glide 1.1 s (flippers held back), recovery 0.5 s; rear flippers waggle ±8° as rudders; two short
current lines peel off each flipper tip on every power stroke. OUTING: swims to a spot, settles (scale 0.97, sand
puff), bites seagrass (head dips, 3 times), strokes toward the top edge to breathe and glides home. Signature: the
breath ring: at the "surface" the head pushes forward 3%, a ring expands from the head, 2 droplets fall.

**leatherback-turtle** (side view, head right, long front flipper below): Deepest-diving reptile (~1,280 m).
Front flippers huge. Starts each dive with strokes at ~0.32 Hz down a steep ~40° descent, then glides; ascent strokes
~0.30 Hz but it rises slowly. Eats jellyfish almost exclusively. PASS: a diagonal dive: enters top left, exits low
right at 30–40°, nose-down to match; first half 2 strokes (flipper region rotates about the shoulder +30° to −40° and
back, ~0.75 s each way); second half flipper swept back, a pure glide, speeding up; light rays from the top edge
shorten and fade with depth. OUTING: a V-dive: glides down, levels, rises slowly with strokes; an ink jellyfish drifts
in; head stretches forward 4%, the jelly disappears, the jaw squeezes twice. Signature: the jellyfish gulp.

**whale-shark** (head right): Largest fish; slow side-to-side tail; negatively buoyant: glides with the tail still
on descents, beats to climb. Filter-feeds mouth open at the surface; in dense prey hangs vertically and suction-gulps.
PASS (5 s): gentle sawtooth: glide down 3% over 1.5 s with the tail still, climb 3% over 1.5 s with a slow tail beat
(S-bend, amplitude growing to the tip, ~1 beat per 2 s); mouth slightly open throughout; plankton specks drift toward
the mouth and vanish at the lip. OUTING: rises to the top, rotates ~70° head-up and hangs there "bottle feeding": the
mouth region expands and closes, once per 2 s, a plankton cloud funnels in; tips back and glides down to its spot
without a tail beat. Signature: the vertical bottle-feeding gulp.

**reef-manta-ray** (top view, head at top, wings left-right): Flaps its pectoral "wings"; average stroke ~0.31 Hz
(~3.2 s per beat); up to ~0.87 body lengths/s; feeding: beats faster but not faster in speed; cephalic fins rolled
up while travelling, unrolled to funnel food; barrel-rolls in vertical loops in dense plankton; hovers almost still
at cleaning stations. PASS: rotate 90° so the head leads; in top view a flap shows as foreshortening: both wing
regions squash toward the body to 80% width with the tips curling, then spread back (~1.5 s each way: slow and
stately); a long gentle sine path banking ±5°; wavy caustic light lines pass over the screen above it. OUTING: glides
into a cloud of plankton specks; the cephalic fins unfurl (splayed 20% wider); 3 barrel rolls in place (each 1.5 s:
the length scales 1 → 0.2 → mirrored → back to 1 while it drifts a little); rolls the head fins back in, glides home.
Its drawing has 3 small bubbles (layer 5) near the right wing tip. Signature: the barrel roll.

**blue-spotted-stingray** (top view, head at top, long tail down): Swims by rippling the disc margins, waves head to
tail along both edges. Seldom buries; hides under ledges with the tail out. Feeds on sand flats on the rising tide
(mostly night), digging pits for molluscs and worms, trapping prey under the disc; goatfish follow it. PASS: rotate so
the head points right, travel low, skimming the bottom; both margin bands carry a travelling wave (1.5 wavelengths
visible, 1 wave per second head to tail); tail trails with a lag and a light sway; a small sand puff at each margin
trough near the bottom. OUTING: settles, flutters its front margin hard (3 quick ripples), a sand cloud erupts; when it
clears, a dark ellipse (the pit) is left; twice in two spots; then slides under a drawn ledge line with only the tail
showing 1 s, then returns. Signature: the disc flutter that blows a sand pit.

**dugong** (head right): Slow seagrass grazer; fluke beats up and down; feeds just above the bed snout down,
ploughing up whole plants, leaving feeding trails; pushes off the bottom with its flippers; surfaces briefly (~2–3 s).
41–58% of time feeding. PASS: low and level, head tilted 10° down; the fluke rotates ±12° about the tail stock once
per 1.5 s, rear body bobs ±1% in phase; a sand plume trails from the snout; a dotted feeding trail along the bottom
behind it. OUTING: grazes along the bottom in small flipper push-off hops (flipper rotates down, body lifts 2%); angles
up ~30° and rises to the top; only the snout tip crosses a surface line, briefly, with a 3-droplet breath puff; sinks
back. Its drawing has small bubbles drawn near the head. Signature: the brief snout breath.

**irrawaddy-dolphin** (head right): Rounded head, no beak, small dorsal fin; slow; up-and-down fluke strokes;
surfaces in a quiet roll (only the top of the head shows); lifts the fluke only for deep dives. Spits water jets up to
~1.5 m, thought to herd or stun fish, often while spyhopping. PASS: two gentle surfacing rolls: a low sine path with
the drawing rotated along its slope (±15°); fluke rotates ±15° about the tail stock, ~1.3 Hz; at each crest the head
touches a drawn surface line and gives a small breath puff. OUTING: swims to a spot, rotates to vertical head up
(spyhop), bobs 3%; spits: an arc of 6–8 ink droplets from the mouth, fish specks scatter where they land; repeats once
in the other direction, rolls back home. Signature: the water-spit arc.

**humpback-whale** (head right, long pectoral fin below): Pectoral fins up to ~1/3 of body length, used to bank and
turn tightly. Slow up-and-down fluke. Breaching, pectoral slaps, lobtailing, fluke-up dives. Bubble nets: a spiral of
bubbles corrals prey, then a vertical lunge with the throat pleats expanded. PASS: slow and massive, on a slow
climbing curve that banks (0 to +8° and back); the pectoral region sweeps ±20° about the shoulder like a wing once
per 2 s, the fluke ±10° once per 2 s, half a cycle out of step with the pectoral; a trail of bubbles from the
blowhole; light rays from above. OUTING: shrunk to ~60% (mirrored heading left), circles a tightening spiral leaving
a ring of rising dot bubbles; lunges up through the ring: rotate 70° head-up, throat region expands 120%, 3 fish specks
vanish; ends with a fluke-up dive back to its spot. Signature: bubble-net spiral and upward lunge.

**scalloped-hammerhead** (head right; the hammer is the scalloped shape at the right, the body tapers to a thin tail
at the left): Side-to-side tail; tagged animals roll constantly, often swimming at ~60° on their side (more at
night); sweeps its head side to side for near-360° vision; big schools round seamounts by day, hunt alone at night.
PASS: straight and smooth, the only shark-like glide in the set; tail band S-wave at 1 Hz; the hammer region rotates
±5° about the neck on its own rhythm (1 sweep per 1.5 s), visibly out of step with the tail; faint concentric sense
arcs pulse from the hammer every 1.5 s. OUTING: patrols a long oval and "rolls" (tilt −10° and squash vertically to
85% for 2 s), drops low and scans the sand with the head, a sense arc lights up, noses down once (sand puff), returns.

**clownfish** (head right): Rows with its pectoral fins (bobbing "waddle"), tail for bursts; rarely strays more
than a few metres from its anemone; wriggles down into the tentacles; chases intruders with pops and clicks. PASS
(3 s): a nervous dash: short spurts (0.5 s fast with tail flicking, 0.25 s hovering) on a slight zigzag; pectorals a
fast flutter; body bobs ±2% with the pectoral rhythm; tiny bubbles. OUTING: stays near home, drawn wavy anemone
tentacles; darts out twice, stops, turns back; third trip a little farther; dives into the anemone: wriggles ±10°
quickly for 0.75 s while the tentacle lines sway over it. Signature: the anemone wiggle-snuggle.

**seahorse** (upright, snout right, tail curled at bottom): Swims upright by beating its dorsal fin at 30–42 Hz
(the popular 70 Hz is not supported), steers with the pectorals; among the slowest fish (dwarf seahorse ~1.5 m/h);
anchors with its prehensile tail; pivot feeding: snaps the head up in ~5 ms on a spring-loaded tendon. PASS: it cannot
cross a screen alone, so it rides a current: current streak lines cross the screen first, it drifts on them tilted 15°
forward, rising and dropping in 2% buoyancy steps; the tail slowly uncurls and recurls; the dorsal fin flickers;
plankton specks travel alongside. OUTING: uncurls its tail from a drawn seagrass stalk, rises straight up, drifts,
stops below a copepod speck, freezes 1 s, snaps (head rotates up 20° in one frame, the speck vanishes, the head
returns), drifts back and curls its tail round the stalk. Signature: the one-frame pivot snap.

**day-octopus** (front view, mantle top, arms below): Hunts by day; crawls over the reef on its arms; escapes by
jetting through its siphon mantle first, arms trailing, often after squirting ink; changes skin colour and texture
instantly; "web-over": spreads arms and web over a coral head to net prey; hunts with groupers and goatfish. PASS: a
jet pulse: rotate 90° so the mantle leads; each 1 s pulse: arms draw together into a streamline over 0.25 s while it
jumps forward 8%, then spread back over 0.75 s as it coasts; a burst of water dashes from the siphon on each
contraction; 4 pulses, strong surge and coast. OUTING: upright, walks along the bottom (alternate arm groups rotate
±10° in turn, body steps 2% per 0.5 s); rises, spreads all arms to 125% width like an umbrella, drops over a drawn
rock, holds 1 s while the arm tips probe; retracts, walks home. Signature: the web-over pounce.

**moon-jellyfish**: Pulses its bell; peak speed at ~0.55 Hz (~11 mm/s); weak swimmer, mostly drifts. Each pulse sheds
two counter-rotating vortex rings (one on contraction, one on relaxation and refill). PASS: rides a current: pulse of
1.5 s: contract 0.25 s (bell squashes to 80% width and 105% height, steps up 3%), relax 1.25 s (bell eases back, sinks
1%); tentacles and oral arms lag the bell, bowing inward then trailing; travels sideways on current lines. OUTING:
drifts; turns by pulsing unevenly (8° per pulse); rises to the top, stops pulsing 2 s and sinks with tentacles
streaming up, pulses home. Signature: two small curl marks spin off the bell rim at each contraction and relaxation
and drift down.

**horseshoe-crab** (top view, shell at top, tail spike down): Walks on legs hidden under the shell, so it seems to
glide. If flipped it digs the telson into the sand and levers itself over, best by rolling to one side (a trackway
shows ≥16 attempts, all to the right). Juveniles swim upside down. PASS: rotate so the head points right; a low,
perfectly level glide along the bottom; legs only a faint jitter; leaves a trackway (two rows of tiny dots and a centre
line where the telson drags); the telson swings ±4° as a rudder. OUTING: crawls out; a surge line sweeps across and
flips it (scale-y 1 → 0 → −1); on its back it pushes with the telson (rotates 25°, digs in, sand puff); two tries fail
(it tilts and falls back); the third, to the right, rolls it upright; glides home. Signature: the telson-lever righting.

**cuttlefish** (head right, fin skirt along the mantle edge): Hovers and creeps with the fin skirt round its mantle;
jets to escape; can drop ink as a cloud or as a pseudomorph (a mucus-ink blob shaped like itself, a decoy); "passing
cloud" dark bands sweep across its skin; catches prey with two shooting tentacles. PASS: first ~2 s the skirt bands
carry a fine travelling wave (3 crests visible, 2 per second), the body gliding dead level (steadier than any fish);
then it jets: the mantle squeezes to 90% briefly and it leaps 25% of the screen in a quarter second; a pseudomorph
stays where it was: a solid ink blob in roughly its own silhouette that slowly falls apart into dots. OUTING: hunts a
shrimp speck: creeps toward it, raises the front arm region 20°, holds 1 s; two ink lines shoot from the arms to the
speck in one frame and snap back, the speck is gone; returns swimming backward without turning, the fin wave reversed.

**lionfish** (head right, fan of venomous spines): Slow hoverer; swim-bladder muscles hold any posture, often slightly
head-down; corners prey with big pectoral fans and blows pulsed water jets at it (on average 18.5 pulses before the
strike); before a group hunt it flares its fins, undulates its tail, then waves one pectoral and then the other. PASS
(5 s): a regal slow procession, tilted 8° head-down, dead straight; pectoral fan regions open and shut ±10° slowly
(once per 2 s); spine tips sway with a lag; plankton specks part around the fins. OUTING: drifts to a group of small
fish specks; fans spread to 115%; signals: waves the left fan, then the right; blows jets: 6 small puffs of current
dashes from the mouth, one every 0.25 s, the specks flinch; strikes: lunges 10% forward in one frame, a speck vanishes;
glides back with fans folded. Signature: puff-puff-puff-STRIKE.

**pufferfish** (head right): Swims with the tail folded, sculling its small rear dorsal and anal fins side to side,
pectorals fluttering; slow and clumsy but can hover and back up; tail only to escape. Inflates by gulping water
repeatedly into a stomach pouch within seconds; deflates by spitting it out. PASS: starts smaller (~60% of screen
height); bumbling: pectorals flutter, dorsal and anal regions alternate quick flicks, tail closed, ±3° yaw wobble;
halfway a shadow passes and it inflates in gulps (mouth open, a lurch, 1.0 → 1.2 → 1.4 → 1.6 → 1.8), round, spines
out; drifts on slowly, tumbling 10°. OUTING: inflates and rises like a balloon; deflates with a water jet from the
mouth that pushes it backward 15%, shrinks back in 3 steps, sculls home. Signature: the gulp-by-gulp inflation.

**bumphead-parrotfish** (head right): Largest parrotfish (~1.3 m); rows with its pectorals, bursts with the tail;
schools bite and headbutt coral, grinding it to sand with throat teeth; each fish eats ~2–5 t of reef a year; a school
returns ~50 t of sand to its reef a year; males ram heads (reported); schools sleep in caves. PASS: steady and
businesslike: the pectoral region rows ±25° about its base, 2 rows/s; level, 5° head-down, the hump leading; every
1.5 s a sand cloud puffs from the vent, trails behind and settles (it is making the beach). OUTING: heads to a drawn
coral branch; rears back, rams forward, the screen jitters; splinter marks fly, the coral loses a piece; chews (head
squeezes 3 times); a big sand cloud follows from behind; rows home. Signature: the headbutt-crunch.

**moray-eel** (head right, long body): Swims by undulating the whole body, waves head to tail growing toward the
tail; most have no pectoral fins. Lives in crevices with the head out, opening and shutting its mouth all the time
(breathing, not a threat). A second set of jaws drags prey in; hunts cooperatively with roving coral groupers. PASS: a
true travelling body wave, the only full-body wave in the set: ~1.2 wavelengths along the body, 1 cycle per second,
amplitude from 1% at the head to 6% at the tail; very little head yaw; the jaw opens and closes every 1.5 s; small
swirl marks at each tail crest. OUTING: starts as just a head poking out of a dark crevice drawn on one side; slides
out, snakes an S-path, pokes its head into 2 drawn holes (head dips, sand puff); returns tail first, backing into the
crevice while the wave runs in reverse. Signature: the breathing gape from the den.

**giant-grouper** (head right): To ~2.7 m, 400 kg; sluggish; hovers near caves and overhangs, steadied by its
pectorals; feeds by suction, engulfing prey whole in an instant; the related goliath grouper booms with its swim
bladder (not confirmed for this species). PASS (5 s): heavy and slow, a lumbering push-and-hold rhythm; tail sweeps
slowly once per 2 s; pectorals flick every 0.5 s; midway it booms: 3 concentric low arcs radiate from the body and the
whole drawing shudders ±1% briefly. OUTING: patrols, pauses at a drawn cave mouth, hovers 2 s; a small fish speck
passes the mouth; in one frame the mouth and gill region swell to 130%, current lines and the speck snap into the
mouth, the drawing lurches 5% forward; returns to normal and booms once, smugly. Signature: the instant suction gulp.

**chambered-nautilus** (shell left, tentacles right): Jet propulsion through a flexible funnel that also steers;
usually swims backward, shell first, ~0.9 body lengths/s; efficient for a jetter; migrates vertically daily (500–600 m
reported), regulating buoyancy with its chambered shell; each jet makes the shell rock. Jet rate ~1/s (estimate). PASS:
travels shell first (so in a left-to-right pass it is mirrored: shell leading to the right); 1 s pulse: the tentacle
region draws together and pulls toward the shell over 0.25 s, the drawing jumps forward ~7% and the shell pitches 8°;
then it extends over 0.75 s while the rock settles; water dashes leave the funnel backward on each pulse. OUTING: the
vertical migration: starting low, rises in slow rocking pulses, tentacles fanned at the top, drifts on its buoyancy
2 s, sinks back with soft pulses. Signature: the rock-and-recoil backward pulse.

**mudskipper** (head right, on a mud line that is part of the drawing): Crutches across mud: both stiff pectoral fins
swing forward and vault the body; skips up to ~60 cm with a flick of the tail; territorial males raise their dorsal
fins, gape and leap; rivals leap at each other; some defend ~2 m of mud. PASS: crutch step of 0.5 s: pectoral region
rotates back 30°, the body vaults 3% forward and 2% up, then drops; after 4 steps a skip: the tail curls, the fish flies
a parabola up to 25% of screen height and lands with a mud splat (5 droplets) and a print; more steps. OUTING: crutches
to the middle of its territory; the dorsal fin (u 0.6–0.72, v 0.15–0.5) flags up (140%) and the mouth gapes; 3 vertical
leaps in place with splats; a mirrored rival flash (the drawing flips once and back) hints at a duel; flicks mud over
itself, crutches home. Signature: the dorsal flag plus the vertical leap.

**fiddler-crab** (front view, huge claw on the right): The male waves his enlarged claw in species-specific patterns
(lateral-circular, lateral-flick, rapid-vertical, circular), ~30% faster near rivals, neighbours sometimes in sync;
walks sideways; feeds with the small claw only, leaving sand pellets; dives into his burrow at any threat. PASS (3 s):
a sideways scuttle (crabs travel sideways, so the front view works directly): quick alternating steps, 1% body
bounce; claw held high and tucked; midway one wave on the move (the claw rotates out 40° about the elbow, up, holds,
drops); sand pellets drop behind. OUTING: mostly in place; the small claw scoops to the mouth 3 times leaving pellets;
then 4 waves, faster each time; a shadow passes, he drops into a burrow drawn below (moves down 20%, scales to 0.8),
eyestalks peek out after a second, he comes back out. Signature: the quickening claw wave.

**barracuda** (head right, slim): Great barracuda adults usually alone, hovering motionless in mid-water or slowly
patrolling; countershaded; ambush hunters with short bursts (43–58 km/h, sources disagree). PASS (3 s): stillness,
then violence: drifts slowly in from the left edge (25% of the screen in 2 s), only the fins flickering; freezes
completely for 1 s; strikes: the tail S-bends once and it crosses the rest of the screen in about 0.4 s with speed
streaks and glints, gone. OUTING: hovers dead still mid-screen, slowly rotates to track a small fish speck that wanders
past, shadows it at a fixed distance stopping when it stops, strikes, the speck is gone, drifts back to its post.
Signature: the freeze followed by the strike.

**sea-star** (top view, five arms): Moves on hundreds of hydraulic tube feet; a slow crawl (feet out of sync) and a
faster bouncing gait (feet in sync); very slow (~10–15 cm/min); no front: any arm can lead, with an eyespot at each arm
tip; humps over a clam to open it. PASS: a time-lapse crossing in the bouncing gait: one arm points along the path,
the drawing pulses 1.00 → 1.03 → 1.00 every 0.5 s (a bounce) with a 1% hop; the leading arm tip lifts and curls; a
dotted track of tube-foot prints trails behind. OUTING: crawls with no bounce, the leading arm tip raised to "look";
changes direction without turning (the drawing stays put while the lifted tip passes to another arm) and sets off at
72°; finds a drawn clam, humps over it (arms arch inward, centre 108%, held 2 s), crawls home with another arm leading.
Signature: switching the leading arm, a new direction with no turn.
